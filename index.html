Trend interpretation principles

  • Look for persistent changes rather than overreacting to one isolated reading.
  • Check measurement technique, device quality and timing before interpreting a result.
  • Combine symptoms and multiple indicators only under validated clinical rules.
  • Never let an app trend override symptoms or mission emergency procedures.

BRAIN & NERVOUS SYSTEM

Behavior, sleep and neurological function

Relevant observations can include sleep patterns, mood, fatigue, balance and visual changes. Concerns require the mission's approved clinical assessment.

Possible methods: questionnaires, actigraphy, clinical neurological and vision examinations.

NASA's five human-spaceflight hazards

Spaceflight hazards interact. A single exposure may affect several body systems.

01 · Space radiation

Ionizing radiation can damage cells and DNA. Monitor dosimeter readings, cumulative exposure and mission radiation alerts. Follow approved shelter and exposure protocols.

02 · Isolation and confinement

Confinement can affect mood, sleep, stress and teamwork. Monitor sleep, fatigue and behavioral-health questionnaires; use scheduled support and recreation.

03 · Distance from Earth

Communication delays and limited supplies increase the need for onboard medical capability, crew training, reliable records and predefined escalation plans.

04 · Altered gravity

Microgravity can contribute to bone and muscle loss, cardiovascular adaptation, balance changes and fluid shifts. Use approved exercise and scheduled examinations.

05 · Hostile closed environments

Constrained living conditions, air quality, contaminants and microorganisms can affect health. Monitor environmental sensors, hygiene and illness reports.

What should be measured?

Routine self-monitoring

Heart rate, blood pressure, temperature, sleep, mood, symptoms, exercise and nutrition observations.

Device and environment data

Personal/spacecraft dosimetry, air quality, carbon dioxide, temperature and other environmental telemetry.

Scheduled medical examinations

Blood and saliva biomarkers, bone-health assessment, vision/ocular examinations, ultrasound and fitness assessments as mission protocols require.

Why combine them?

A single vital sign cannot establish bone density, immune function, eye structure or overall medical status. Self-monitoring complements—not replaces—clinical examinations.

Mission-safe health guidance

Routine: Continue scheduled monitoring and approved preventive routines when measurements are complete and no concerning symptoms or alerts are present.
Attention: Follow the mission-approved repeat-measurement procedure and contact the designated medical team when a result is outside personal approved limits, a trend is unusual or symptoms persist.
Urgent: For severe, rapidly worsening or potentially life-threatening symptoms, follow the spacecraft emergency checklist and contact the onboard/ground medical authority immediately. Do not wait for this app.

Preventive health principles

  • Follow mission-prescribed exercise and nutrition plans.
  • Maintain a consistent sleep routine where possible.
  • Use radiation monitoring and shelter instructions from mission control.
  • Follow hygiene and infection-control procedures.
  • Attend scheduled medical examinations and report changes honestly.
  • Use only mission-approved medication and treatment protocols under qualified medical oversight.
This prototype does not diagnose, prescribe, calculate clinical risk, establish medical thresholds or replace a flight surgeon. Any real deployment requires clinical validation, human-factors testing, cybersecurity review and approval by the responsible space agency.
